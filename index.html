Initial Opinion on Mechanical Drawings

I was working on my thesis project one morning when I suddenly realized, "my raspberry Pi won't last 5 minutes outside in the winter". CAD, specifically OnShape & SOLIDWORKS, has been on my mind recently (a lot) so the first thing that made sense was to design my own enclosure! (see below)

This design was OK200 but it wasn't done. I have plenty of changes in mind to make to it to have it be ready for everything the outside world will put it thru. Sadly, it took me about 6 hours to develop this initial design - far long than it would have taken me in my CAD prime during my first year @North Central College - and, quite frankly, the thot of making more changes to this design irritated me. I thot, "what if I ask Copilot to help me?"

As usual, the response was optimistic. The initial response led me to believe that I could directly upload .STL or .STP files to Copilot and I could ask it for recommendations. Unfortunately, 2 inquiries later I learned that this was not the case:

So.. Copilot said I could upload screen-grabs!

Now I had a different problem, previously I've had a CAD prime but I've never had a mechanical drawings prime - I've never made a mechanical drawing. Thanks to my time working in CAD I'd at least noticed the "drawings" button in the software. After a another 5 or so hours of clicking and playing around with the feature I came up with this:

It's definitely not finished, but, I had other tasks to attend to so that's where I left it before staring this blog post. I thot, "what did I just do, what did I do this for, and what is this good for?" My answer - I just build documentation to help me communicate with an AI. So, referencing the topic of this post, my initial opinion on mechanical drawings is that they are good for communicating. When I used the feature it gave me the option to import a drawing from a CAD design. It also let me import measurements based on the CAD drawing. Making this drawing felt like it was naturally supposed to come after the CAD design! This makes me feel like it's alright to designs something that I haven't yet been able to describe on paper, because I can describe it on paper after it's been designed. If this is the case, then mechanical drawings are good for communicating your idea after you've designed it. It displays measurements, different orientations, materials, titles, and other important information - all on one document! If I had a team, I could give them the drawing and they could view it on their cellphone and understand what my enclosure design looks like, what it is made of, and how it works.

In conclusion, thru this work I've convinced myself that mechanical drawings, often times called engineering drawings, are useful for communicating designs with team members & AIs. This opinion was formed after my first time using CAD software to make a drawing. This, however, is not my first time making drawings. I used to frequently make mechanical drawings while an undergrad in engineering, but I would make these drawings using pen and paper!! Then, when I learned CAD, I would take my detailed hand drawn artifacts and quickly layout appropriatly-shaped planes which I could cut and extrude to create my designs. Now, after a few years of being out of practice I've developed a design before the drawing. In the past, my drawings were useful for communicating to myself what I want to create. Now, they have become useful for communicating to others what I have created. A lesson learned is that I should` learn to create drawings in CAD software :_)

Investigating Unrecognized Network Devices

I was snooping in my router's settings when I noticed a couple devices I couldn't recall adding to my network.

Here's what my router's management page looks like.

I was snooping around it when I noticed 2 devices I didn't recognize - "192.168.1.242 / Linux" and "192.168.1.111 / espressif"

I didn't want to risk the Chinese government being in my home network so I launched an investigation. The first step was to see if they had any open ports. The espressif device had port 80 open. The Linux device has port 6668 open and 27 filterd ports.

Next, I wanted to see if they responded to a basic CURL request. Espressif did, but Linux timed-out.

Here's what the espressif device returned on a web-browser.

This information was nice but I still had no clue what devices they were. A quick Google search revealed that the MAC addresses for those devices were registered to ESPressif Inc - a huge China-based MCU designer - and the Linux devices was registered to TUYA (another big IoT device company). This was insightful, but I still didn't know what devices they were.

I blocked their MAC addresses in my router's configuration and nothing stopped working at home, so it couldn't have been anything I set-up. I flicked on and off all of my lightbulbs and smart speakers but they wouldn't dissapear form the network.

It wasn't until after I disconnected my siblings water kettle that the espressif device disconnected from the network - myster half solved! It turned out the water kettle was a smart device added to the network without my knowledge.

I still didn't know what the Linux device was. It's signal strength was -85mdB - super weak - and besides for it's MAC and IP address I didn't know anything else about it. I wanted to figure out how far from the router it could be.

Finally, I connected my cellphone to the router and walked away from the router. I ended up going outside, into the backyard, past the patio, and sat in a chair next to a firepit - almost off my proprety. My signal strength read "-81mdB"! Even though I was almost off my property I still had a stronger signal than the Linux devices. Going into my neighbors yard/house was off the table so, with nothing else to do, I settled for simply blocking it's MAC address and hiding my network SSID. As long as I regularly monitor for the reappearance of this device my network should remain uncompromised.

Benchmarking Hallucination Detection

Have you ever read a response from your AI and thot "this is totally not what I was talking about.... but wait, this bit (& this bit only) is exactly what I was talking about".

Read the heading. This is my first post. The topic of this heading is the most common hallucination I've encountered. When this happens it is important to remember that AI is ONLY a tool for you (the sophisticated one) to leverage. This is your opportunity to grab the AI by the handle-bars and steer it in the appropriate direction. Start a brand new chat on a newly grounded (& more evolved) topic.
